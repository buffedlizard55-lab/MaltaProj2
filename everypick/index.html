How a pick is produced

  1. Real prices first. Two-way bookmaker and exchange prices are converted to no-vig probabilities (each book's two implied probabilities normalised to 100%), then averaged. Every book price, its decimal odds and its overround is printed on the row.
  2. Official ratings only when no book prices the match. Valve's published VRS points go through p = 1/(1+exp(-k·C·Δpoints)) with Valve's own C = 0.0056000078 and this project's fitted k = 0.4308 (re-fitted here: best k on a 60-pair grid is 0.448, MAE 7.70 pp).
  3. One-sided ratings become bounds. If a team has no row in Valve's table, its chance is at or below Valve's published floor, so the row shows ≥ / ≤ instead of pretending to a point estimate.
  4. Nothing is guessed. A fixture that fits none of the above is published as unresolved with the attempt ledger. Right now that list is empty and the limitations are listed on Unresolved & limitations.
Read this before betting a pick. Market rows are consensus prices; originated rows are models with a measured error of about ±8 pp, and three fixtures rest on a single unrated opponent, so they are directional. Details and the conflicting-signal rows are on Unresolved & limitations and Flagged for review.
