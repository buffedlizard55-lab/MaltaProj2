Contrarian, but not reckless

  1. Keep the best-supported win probability separate from a contest strategy. The default pick remains the higher eligible probability. Choosing an underdog to increase leaderboard variance does not make that team more likely to win.
  2. Require a dated pre-lock case. Start with external two-sided, no-vig prices. Check confirmed starters and stand-ins, recent maps against comparable opponents, veto and format. Use schedule/rest only when dates and travel evidence support it. Avoid counting news twice if the market has already repriced it.
  3. Distinguish scoring goals. For equal reward per correct answer, expected points favor the higher probability. If rewards differ, compare probability × reward using verified rules. Climbing a top-heavy leaderboard is a different objective requiring the scoring, current position, remaining fixtures and rivals’ selections. No precise leaderboard-optimal pick is claimed without them.
  4. Do not learn a betting rule from one upset. Log the source, forecast and lock time before each match; grade later with calibration/Brier score across many matches. A post-match explanation is not a winning backtest.

Policy recommendation: keep a small, explicitly labelled research watchlist of credible underdogs rather than force a contrarian pick on every slate. Do not override featured market picks with unsupported roster narratives. HotSpawn popularity is not used as a win-probability input.
