Method & sources

1 · Where the matches come from

The HotSpawn pick'em lobby (play.hotspawn.com/lobby) is a server-rendered Angular app. Its public script#ng-state block carries the exact /api/v1/fixtures response the page renders. We read that block, keep the CS2 rows the lobby actually renders, and keep only the rows still ahead of their lock. The direct API is also read so fixtures added after the page cache was built are covered, but an API-only row is never treated as pickable — the API carries the whole history and reports long-finished fixtures as "scheduled".

HotSpawn supplies fixtures and settlement only. Its crowd pick percentages are not collected and are never a probability input, because the brief requires lines from outside HotSpawn.

2 · Where the prices come from

Every price is read from an external source, in this order:

  1. Pinnacle — licensed bookmaker, public guest odds API.
  2. Bovada — licensed bookmaker, public coupon JSON.
  3. Melbet (1xBet platform) — licensed bookmaker, public LineFeed JSON. This is the feed that actually prices most tier-B and tier-C CS2.
  4. Kalshi — CFTC-regulated exchange. Kept only when both sides quote a bid and an ask and each side's bid-ask spread is at most 10 cents.
  5. Official Valve regional standings — the only official team rating used, and only where no book prices the match.

3 · How a price becomes a probability

For decimal prices dA and dB the implied chances are 1/dA and 1/dB. The book margin is removed proportionally: pA = (1/dA) / ((1/dA) + (1/dB)). Where more than one book prices a match, the published number is the plain arithmetic mean of the per-source no-vig probabilities. A quote is joined only when both team names resolve to the HotSpawn teams through a reviewed alias record and the kickoffs agree to within five minutes. Where a book lists the pair in the opposite order, the orientation is reversed and the row says so.

4 · The originated line

Where no book prices a match and both teams sit on a single row of Valve's official regional standings (snapshot 2026_10_05), the probability comes from Valve's own model constants, published in ValveSoftware/counter-strike_regional_standings: q = ln(10)/400, g = 1/sqrt(1 + 3·q²·RD²/π²) with the repository default RD = 75, and p = sigmoid(k · g · ln(10)/400 · (pointsA − pointsB)).

Valve's own README states the published curve is too steep at the ends, so the slope k is fitted on this capture: least squares through the origin of logit(market probability) against C·(pointsA − pointsB), over every row that has both a market consensus and two unique official ratings. This capture: 4 qualifying pairs, k = 1.0 (Valve default, too few pairs), mean absolute error None at that k versus None at Valve's own slope. k is clamped to [0.25, 1.5].

An originated line is ours. It is not a bookmaker price, not an official forecast, and it has no claimed out-of-sample accuracy. It is labelled on every page it appears on.
