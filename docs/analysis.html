Method in one line

Our line = normalise(1/odds) for each source, then an equal-weight average across the sources that published a two-sided price for that fixture. Hotspawn's crowd percentage is deliberately excluded.

Structural findings

  • Coverage is uneven by tier. All four Tier S Thunderpick fixtures carry a bc.game line (three of the four also have a Polymarket market). The six Tier C Elisa BreakOut Group B fixtures rely on Polymarket only, all with $0 volume, and are marked Low quality; Group A has a bc.game line for five of its six fixtures. 15 of 26 rows in total carry a bc.game line and 20 carry a Polymarket market.
  • The 8:00 pm ESL Challenger League final is a live example of crowd-vs-market divergence. The crowd is 86% on ex-Keyd Stars while the prediction market prices Galorys at 68c; the bc.game line for that fixture was not captured, so the row carries only the prediction-market line and is flagged.
  • Naming differences are systematic, not one-off. Hotspawn uses WROTBERRY/RoundsGG/EAC where HLTV writes WBT/ROUNDS/EAC and Polymarket writes WBT/RoundsGG/Esport Academy Copenhagen. Fixture slots and kickoff times line up exactly, but the identity mapping is flagged for human confirmation.
  • Odds drift within a single page. EGamersWorld displayed 3.6/1.27 for 9z vs Aurora in its odds module and 3.8/1.22 in the schedule list of the same page. Both are recorded; only one is used in the derivation.
  • De-margining matters in Tier C. On the most liquid fixtures the book margin is under 5%, so the difference between raw and de-margined probability is small; on thin markets the raw and de-margined numbers can move by several points, which is why the raw method is documented on the Methodology page.
