Structural findings

  • Coverage is uneven by tier. All four Tier S Thunderpick fixtures carry a bc.game line (three also have a Polymarket market). The Elisa BreakOut fixtures have bc.game lines for all twelve fixtures (captured on re-check) but the Polymarket side of Group B is $0-volume, so those rows lean on the bookmaker module. 22 of 26 rows carry a bc.game line (all 26 fixtures except the two ESL Challenger League rows, ex-RUSTEC vs mellren and FORZE Reload vs LFO 10) and 20 carry a Polymarket market; 17 carry both.
  • The ESL Challenger League playoffs are a live example of crowd-vs-market divergence. The crowd is 86% on ex-Keyd Stars while the prediction market prices Galorys at 68c; no two-sided book line existed for either ESL fixture, so both rows carry only the prediction-market line and are flagged as single-source.
  • Naming differences are systematic, not one-off. Hotspawn uses WROTBERRY/RoundsGG/EAC where HLTV writes WBT/ROUNDS/EAC and Polymarket writes WBT/RoundsGG/Esport Academy Copenhagen. EGamersWorld's own team URL for the roster is /counterstrike/team/wrotberry-...
  • Odds drift within a single page. EGamersWorld displayed 3.6/1.27 for 9z vs Aurora in its odds module and 3.8/1.22 in the schedule list of the same page. Both are recorded; only one is used in the derivation.
  • De-margining matters in Tier C. On the most liquid fixtures the book margin is under 5%, so the difference between raw and de-margined probability is small; on thin markets the raw and de-margined numbers can move by several points, which is why the raw method is documented on the Methodology page.
