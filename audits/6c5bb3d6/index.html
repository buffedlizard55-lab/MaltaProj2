The upset in numbers: 9z 2-0 Aurora

  • Ownership: only 4% of the HotSpawn field had 9z at the pick lock (96% Aurora). Verified from the pre-lock board capture at the exact commit the backtest cites (commit 8f66ce4, board generated 2026-10-14T04:55:36Z, retrieved through the GitHub API in this session). The 7-point match therefore paid 7 points to 4% of players and zero to 96%.
  • Price: Aurora were the market favourite; Valve's official standings of 2026-10-05 rank Aurora #9 (1716 pts) and 9z #17 (1543 pts) — a 173-point gap, i.e. about 74% for Aurora on the 400-point logistic scale. The result was a 2-0 the other way.
  • How it happened: 13-11 on 9z's Inferno, then 13-1 on Aurora's own Nuke pick. luchov finished 38-20 with a 1.61 rating, 97.4 ADR and 86.8% KAST; Aurora's IGL kyxsan finished 9-29 at 0.53.
  • Ranking effect: HLTV records 9z gaining 62 VRS points from the win.
  • Why it was not random: Aurora are three months into a full rebuild — Jimpphat and coach ash on 2026-07-03, kyxsan as IGL on 2026-07-08, replacing MAJ3R, soulfly and coach Fabre. Their own coach said in August that practice was good but official matches were not, naming communication and cultural integration.

Climbing the leaderboard: contrarian but not stupid

In a pick'em scored against other players, taking side X with win probability p when a fraction c of the field also took X moves your expected point margin by points × (p − c). So the field-beating pick is the side whose win probability exceeds its ownership — not simply the biggest dog. Recomputed here independently for every open fixture:

What this says, plainly

  • 13 plays carry real leaderboard edge (≥ +0.50 expected points versus the field): Fire Flux in BakS vs Fire Flux (+1.36 pts), antidaun in antidaun vs Phantom Academy (+1.34 pts), BASEMENT BOYS in aimclub vs BASEMENT BOYS (+1.10 pts), GenOne in GenOne vs Butterfly (+1.03 pts), WROTBERRY in Nexus vs WROTBERRY (+1.03 pts), Insiders in Infinite vs Insiders (+1.03 pts), Just Players in BET-M vs Just Players (+0.95 pts), Donstu in Bebop vs Donstu (+0.94 pts), Enjoy in Banda chuya vs Enjoy (+0.82 pts), ASTRAL in ASTRAL vs Leo (+0.73 pts), HAVU in Phantom vs HAVU (+0.73 pts), Honvéd in Honvéd vs Lavked (+0.71 pts), Drama in BC.Game vs Drama (+0.63 pts). All of them are also the published strategy layer's picks — the two methods agree where it matters most, on the 7-point S-tier matches.
  • The three 7-point matches are where a leaderboard is won or lost. 9z (edge — pts), BetBoom and Legacy are all priced below their ownership. Day 1 proved the mechanism: 96% of the field took Aurora and scored 0 on a 7-point match.
  • 7 rows diverge from pure differential math, all of them 2-point tier-C matches where the crowd over-owns a heavy favourite (for example 98% on Phantom against a 67% market price). The published layer deliberately stays chalk there: its rules cap swings per day and only fade near coin-flips or with verified context. The forgone edge is real but small (≤ +0.61 pts per row) and the variance is large — that is the "not stupid" guard, and this audit quantifies it instead of hiding it.
  • Where the official rankings and the market disagree, both are shown. Valve's standings imply BetBoom ≈74% over PARIVISION and Legacy ≈64% over FURIA, while the market prices those matches near even. Five such divergences are listed on the flagged page. Divergence is treated as information, never averaged away silently.
